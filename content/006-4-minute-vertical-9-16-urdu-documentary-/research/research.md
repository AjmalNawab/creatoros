# Research: 4 minute vertical 9:16 Urdu documentary-style explainer : why a dead persons finger usually cannot unlock a phone fingerprint sensor, how fingerprints and fingerprint sensors work, and the surprising cases when it can

A 4-minute journey: how fingerprints form before birth and why even identical twins differ; how phone sensors read them (optical photo, capacitive electricity, ultrasonic sound); why a dead finger usually fails (no blood flow, cooling, drying and wrinkling skin, loss of conductance, plus phone rules like the iPhone's 48-hour passcode rule); and the twist that a very recently deceased finger can sometimes still work, which is why police have even tried 3D-printed fingertips.

**Audience:** Curious Pakistani smartphone users aged 15-35 on Facebook who wonder how everyday tech works

## Facts

| # | Fact | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fingerprint ridges form before birth, mostly between about the 13th and 19th week of pregnancy, and stay the same for life. | medium | MedlinePlus Genetics; Live Science; HowStuffWorks |
| 2 | Even identical twins, who share the same genes, have different fingerprints, because tiny conditions in the womb (position, finger growth, contact with fluid) shape the fine details. | high | Live Science; MedlinePlus Genetics |
| 3 | Most smartphone fingerprint sensors identify a finger through electrical conductance rather than by taking a photo. | high | Live Science (Anil Jain, Michigan State University) |
| 4 | A capacitive sensor is a grid of tiny cells; fingerprint ridges touch the sensor and give a stronger electrical reading than the valleys, which leave a small air gap, so the chip builds a map of the print. | high | Capacitive fingerprint sensor patents (US4353056, US6016355) |
| 5 | Optical fingerprint sensors light up the finger and take a picture of it; they are cheaper and can sit under the screen. | high | Android Authority: How fingerprint scanners work |
| 6 | Capacitive sensors use an array of extremely tiny capacitors that each hold a small electrical charge, and they are harder to fool than simple optical ones. | high | Android Authority: How fingerprint scanners work |
| 7 | Ultrasonic sensors, found mainly in premium phones, send sound waves into the finger and measure the echo to build a 3D map of ridges and pores. | high | Android Authority / iFixit: How ultrasonic fingerprint sensors work |
| 8 | Many sensors also try to check that a finger is real and alive (liveness detection), for example by its electrical or physical properties. | medium | arXiv review: Fingerprint-based authentication, liveness detection (2020); capacitive sensor patents |
| 9 | Fingerprint expert Anil Jain says it gets more difficult to unlock a phone with fingertips the longer a person has been dead. | high | Live Science |
| 10 | Jain explained that without blood flow the fingers can start to wrinkle or shrivel, which changes the print the sensor sees. | medium | Live Science / Fortune coverage quoting Anil Jain |
| 11 | Jain also said that if a person has only recently died, it is possible to unlock a phone with their finger. | high | Live Science |
| 12 | Scientists are not sure exactly how long after death the skin stops conducting electricity well enough for a sensor. | medium | Live Science |
| 13 | iPhones ask for the passcode instead of Touch ID if the phone has not been unlocked for more than 48 hours, after a restart, or after five failed fingerprint attempts. | high | Apple Support: If Touch ID isn't working; Apple Platform Security guide |
| 14 | In 2016, US police asked Jain's lab to make 3D-printed fingertip copies from a murder victim's fingerprints to try to unlock his phone. | high | NPR / WBUR (2016) |
| 15 | Your fingerprint pattern itself does not disappear at death; the problem for phone sensors is the skin's changing electrical and physical condition. | medium | HowStuffWorks; Live Science |

- Naming specific criminal cases or victims sensationally
- Claiming all phones work the same way (optical and ultrasonic sensors also exist)
