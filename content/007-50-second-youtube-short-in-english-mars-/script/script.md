# Script: 50 second YouTube Short in English: Mars Polar Lander, lost on December 3 1999 when its engines shut off about 40 metres above Mars because leg-deployment vibration was read as touchdown, and what NASA changed afterwards

**Title options**
1. Its Engines Shut Off 40 Metres Above Mars
2. The Mars Lander Killed by One Software Glitch
3. Why NASA's Mars Polar Lander Fell From the Sky

### Scene 1: Hook
*Visual: Painted lander descending over the Martian south pole; altimeter HUD frozen at 40 m; engines cut.*

Forty metres above Mars, its engines simply switched off.

### Scene 2: The descent
*Visual: Real NASA launch photo, then the painted lander under its parachute; date tag 3 Dec 1999.*

On December third, 1999, NASA's Mars Polar Lander began its final descent.

### Scene 3: No one listening
*Visual: Painted mission control at night, NO SIGNAL on the big screen.*

Nobody on Earth was listening. The landing was never designed to send live data.

### Scene 4: Legs deploy
*Visual: Lander under parachute, three legs swing down; small cyan blueprint callout of a leg.*

Under its parachute, the lander's three legs snapped out into position.

### Scene 5: False touchdown
*Visual: Blueprint callout: touchdown sensor flickers red for an instant; label SPURIOUS SIGNAL.*

That jolt made the touchdown sensors flicker, just for a moment.

### Scene 6: The software remembered
*Visual: A memory register lights up and stays lit: TOUCHDOWN = TRUE.*

The software should have ignored it. Instead, it remembered.

### Scene 7: Forty metres
*Visual: Altimeter counts down 1500 to 40 m; sensing ON; the stored flag triggers.*

At forty metres, sensing switched on, and the lander believed it had landed.

### Scene 8: Engines off
*Visual: Thruster plumes fade; lander drops; speed readout ~50 mph / 22 m/s.*

Engines off. It likely hit the ground at about fifty miles per hour.

### Scene 9: The real cause
*Visual: Report page icon: MOST PROBABLE CAUSE, software design and testing.*

Investigators concluded the deeper cause was software design and testing.

### Scene 10: Payoff
*Visual: Painted Phoenix lander standing safely on icy northern plains, 2008.*

Its twin later flew as Phoenix, and this time it touched down safely.
