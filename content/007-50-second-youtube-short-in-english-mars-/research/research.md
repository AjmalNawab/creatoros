# Research: 50 second YouTube Short in English: Mars Polar Lander, lost on December 3 1999 when its engines shut off about 40 metres above Mars because leg-deployment vibration was read as touchdown, and what NASA changed afterwards

NASA's Mars Polar Lander launched on 3 January 1999 and was lost on 3 December 1999 as it descended toward Mars' south polar region; no signal was ever received. JPL's Special Review Board (March 2000) concluded the most probable cause was premature shutdown of the descent engines: when the landing legs deployed during parachute descent, their touchdown sensors produced a brief false 'touchdown' signal that the software wrongly remembered, so when touchdown sensing was enabled at about 40 metres altitude the engines were cut and the lander likely hit the surface at about 50 mph (about 22 m/s). The board called the underlying cause inadequate software design and system testing. The cancelled 2001 sister lander later became Phoenix, which landed safely near Mars' north pole in 2008 after a long list of corrective actions.

## Avoid
- Saying the cause is certain (the board said 'most probable'; there is no telemetry)
- Blaming named individuals
- Confusing it with Mars Climate Orbiter's metric/imperial units error (a different mission, same year)
- Claiming the wreck has been found
