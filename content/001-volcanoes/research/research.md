# Research Report: Volcanoes, How Do They Erupt?

Project: 001-volcanoes · Format: 2-min YouTube explainer (Urdu) · Audience: general · Date: 2026-11-20

## Key facts used in the script
| # | Fact | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Earth has layers: crust (thin, ~5–70 km), mantle, core. | High | USGS |
| 2 | Magma is molten or partly molten rock below the surface, typically ~700–1,200 °C. It is called **lava** once it erupts. | High | USGS VHP |
| 3 | Magma is less dense than surrounding rock, so it rises and collects in magma chambers. | High | USGS VHP |
| 4 | Magma holds dissolved gases (mainly water vapour, CO₂, SO₂). As magma rises, pressure drops, gases form bubbles, pressure builds, and the result is an eruption (like opening a soda bottle). | High | USGS VHP |
| 5 | Thick, sticky (silica-rich) magma traps gas, which causes explosive eruptions (e.g. Mount St. Helens, USA, 18 May 1980). | High | USGS |
| 6 | Runny (basaltic) magma lets gas escape, which causes gentle lava flows (e.g. Kīlauea, Hawaiʻi). | High | USGS HVO |
| 7 | About 1,350 potentially active volcanoes worldwide (not counting seafloor ridges). | High | USGS |
| 8 | Most active volcanoes lie around the Pacific "Ring of Fire" (~75% of the world's volcanoes). | Medium-High | USGS / NatGeo |
| 9 | Pakistan: **Koh-e-Sultan** (Chagai, Balochistan) is an ancient volcano with no historical eruptions. | Medium | Smithsonian GVP |
| 10 | Volcanic ash breaks down into fertile soil; eruptions build new land (Hawaiʻi, Iceland). | High | USGS / NatGeo |
