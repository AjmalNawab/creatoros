# Research: Facebook Reel, about 60 seconds, Urdu: K2 in Pakistan is shorter than Everest but far more dangerous. Why the Savage Mountain is deadlier (steeper, more technical, worse and less predictable weather, further north, avalanches, the Bottleneck), the last 8000 m peak climbed in winter, and a respectful nod to Pakistani climbers. Entertaining surprising-fact tone. Use real licensed footage. Platforms: facebook

K2 (8,611 m), on Pakistan's border in Gilgit-Baltistan's Karakoram range, is about 240 m shorter than Everest (8,849 m) but far more dangerous, which is why climbers call it the Savage Mountain. Per summit, K2's death rate has historically been many times Everest's: close to 1 death for every 4 summits until about 2000, and still roughly 13-14% by 2022, against roughly 1-4% on Everest. It is steeper and more technical, its weather is harsher and less predictable, and most deaths happen during or just after the summit push (including the Bottleneck). It was the last of the world's fourteen 8,000 m peaks to be climbed in winter: ten Nepali climbers reached the top on 16 January 2021. Pakistan's Muhammad Ali Sadpara, from Skardu, the only Pakistani to have climbed eight of the 14 eight-thousanders and the first to climb Nanga Parbat in winter, lost his life on K2 in February 2021.

**Audience:** Pakistani families and young people who love the northern areas and enjoy surprising facts about their own country

- Sensationalising deaths; mention Ali Sadpara with respect
- Saying K2 is the most dangerous mountain overall (Annapurna's rate is sometimes higher)
- Politics about the border
- Using climber footage as if it were a specific named expedition
