# Research: 9 minute YouTube documentary in English: the Shenzhou-20 near-miss (November 2025), when a suspected debris strike cracked the returning capsule window, the crew came home on Shenzhou-21 and China launched an uncrewed Shenzhou-22 as a lifeboat; how China's crewed program handles lifeboats and risk, leading to Shenzhou-24 carrying Pakistan's first astronaut to Tiangong. Platforms: youtube

In November 2025 the Shenzhou-20 crew (commander Chen Dong) were about to leave China's Tiangong space station when, during final checks, Chen Dong spotted what looked like a leaf on the return capsule's window. It was a set of cracks, most likely from an impact by a tiny piece of space debris, and later the crew said some cracks had penetrated through the outer pane. The 5 November return was postponed; on 14 November the crew came home in the Shenzhou-21 capsule instead, leaving the newly arrived Shenzhou-21 crew without a safe ride home. China launched the uncrewed Shenzhou-22 around 25 November 2025 as a replacement lifeboat, carrying a porthole crack repair device; a spacewalk in December photographed the damage, and Shenzhou-20 returned uncrewed on 19 January 2026. A similar lifeboat swap happened on the ISS in 2022-23 when Soyuz MS-22 sprang a coolant leak and Soyuz MS-23 was launched empty to replace it. The lesson: crewed stations depend on always having a healthy lifeboat docked, and debris risk is growing. Shenzhou-24, scheduled for 25 November 2026, carries Pakistan's Muhammad Zeeshan Ali as the first foreign visitor to Tiangong.

## Avoid
- Saying the crew was in immediate danger of dying; they were never stranded without options for long and no one was hurt
- Stating a debris size as fact (sources disagree)
- Saying Shenzhou-24 launched or docked before it is confirmed
- Political framing (space race, military) or speculation beyond official statements
- Calling Ali the first Pakistani in space (Namira Salim flew suborbital in 2023)
