A cracked window left three astronauts without a safe ride home, so China launched an empty spaceship to rescue them. Here is how a space-station lifeboat system handles a near-miss.

In November 2025, Shenzhou-20 commander Chen Dong found cracks in the window of his return capsule, most likely caused by a tiny piece of space debris. Some cracks went right through the pane. The crew flew home in the newer Shenzhou-21 instead, which left the next crew on China's Tiangong station without a certified lifeboat until an uncrewed Shenzhou-22 arrived about eleven days later. The International Space Station faced the same problem in 2022 and 2023, when a leaking Soyuz was replaced by an empty one.

We follow what officials and the crews reported, why a damaged window is a hard engineering question, how the lifeboat swap works, and why Shenzhou-24, which carried Pakistan's first astronaut to Tiangong on 25 November 2026, is planned the way it is.
