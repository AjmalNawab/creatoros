# Win the first 90 days with volume

Both accounts are stuck for the same reason: the recommendation systems have not started testing them yet. They have not tested them and found them wanting. The fastest legitimate fix is the same on both platforms: post many fresh, clearly original, tightly hooked short videos on a fixed schedule, and send real shares to interested people. On **Facebook**, Chai & Curiosity should move to **45–90 second Urdu Reels, 4 a week at about 9 pm Pakistan time**, built on the "why does this famous thing…?" pattern that powers Pakistan's biggest explainer channel. It should share each Reel's link (not the file) on WhatsApp, and turn on Meta's AI label because the narration is synthetic. On **YouTube**, Margin of Safety should publish **one 10–20 minute documentary every one to two weeks plus 2–3 original Shorts a week**, each Short linked to its documentary. The topic calendar should be timed to two big round anniversaries: Apollo 1's 60th (27 Jan 2027) and Zeebrugge's 40th (6 Mar 2027). Treat money as a later problem. Standard YouTube monetization works in Pakistan, but the bar doubles for anyone applying from 1 Feb 2027. Facebook's payout program is invite-only and in practice closed to Pakistani bank accounts, so Chai & Curiosity is an audience-building asset, not a revenue source, for now. Expect weeks to a few months and 10–30+ uploads before either feed tests the content reliably.

## Neither account has been tested by the feed yet

On Facebook, a Reel is ranked separately for each viewer from a small shortlist. Meta's system first picks **roughly 10–100 candidate Reels per viewer**, drawn from accounts the viewer follows plus "creators you may not follow" whose content resembles what the viewer already watches. It then predicts how long the viewer will watch, whether they will finish, whether they will skip or hide it, and **whether they will share it to WhatsApp or Messenger**. One of the named inputs is "whether the reel is original content" ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-reels/)). A Page with 1 follower and no viewing history rarely makes those shortlists. So **3–5 impressions per Reel means the Reel barely entered the pool.** It does not mean viewers saw it and rejected it. Meta publishes nothing about a "new Page testing period", so nobody outside Meta knows how many impressions a new Page should expect. Meta's own benchmark for a serious Reels creator is in its Creator Fast Track rules: **15 Reels in 30 days, spread over at least 10 different days** ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/creator-fast-track-grow-your-audience-earn-money-on-facebook/)). That program is not open to this Page, but its cadence is the right target.

YouTube describes its Shorts feed the same way: the system "goes and finds a seed audience" and widens distribution if those viewers respond. Posting more does not itself raise or lower reach, but low-quality volume can hurt ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)). Getting views only from search is good news in one respect: the titles and descriptions work. It also means the feed has not yet given the Shorts a real test. The 71% "stayed to watch" figure is flattered by search traffic, because searchers chose the video on purpose. Feed viewers will swipe more, so the opening second has to work harder. YouTube publishes no benchmark for this metric.

The best large dataset on new channels backs a "many tries" approach. vidIQ followed 448,000 channels created in the first half of 2025. A typical new channel's Shorts got about **1,300 views each in their first 30 days**, flat from the 1st to the 10th upload. Long-form started at a median of **180 views** and rose about 51% by the 10th video. The real wins came from the best video in the batch. **48% of Shorts channels had a 10,000-view hit within their first ten uploads**, against 31% of long-form channels ([vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)). In vidIQ's words, "volume buys you more tries." Anecdotal reports since September 2025 also suggest the Shorts feed now favors fresh uploads over old ones ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/05/youtube-shorts-algorithm-change-new-uploads/)). That makes a steady flow of new Shorts more valuable than hoping old ones resurface.

The signals that move both systems are the ones each platform names. The first is how many people stop scrolling in the first 1–3 seconds. The second is how much of the video they watch and whether they finish or rewatch. The third is real shares. The fourth is originality. None of them can be bought legitimately, and none depends on follower count. That is why a new account can break out, but only after enough attempts.

## AI tools are allowed; mass-produced sameness is not

**YouTube** renamed its "repetitious content" rule to **"inauthentic content"** on 15 July 2025. The rule makes channels that are "mass-produced, generic, repetitive" or that look "made with a template" ineligible for monetization. AI-made content is explicitly not banned in itself; the target is "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). A July 2026 clarification added "off-putting or distressing" content as a separate non-monetizable category ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). That matters for disaster stories: Margin of Safety's calm "near-miss and the fix" framing is safer than tragedy-forward or graphic storytelling. No official text says a synthetic narrator triggers the rule. The real risks are an identical scene template in every video, thin scripts, and very high volume. The channel's protection is a visibly unique researched script for each video, scene designs built for that video, and sources cited on screen and in the description.

YouTube's **"altered or synthetic content" toggle** is needed only for content that looks **realistic**. Examples are a real person shown saying something they didn't, real footage altered, or "a realistic scene that didn't actually occur." Fully animated or clearly stylized work, AI-drafted scripts and AI thumbnails need no disclosure. Disclosing does not reduce reach or monetization, and creators who repeatedly fail to disclose can be penalized ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Painted and blueprint-style scenes therefore need no label. Any photorealistic AI image of a real event, such as a fake "photo" of a lander crash, does need one. The simple rule: toggle "yes" whenever a realistic-looking AI depiction of a real event or person appears.

**Facebook** is stricter on voice. Meta requires its **"AI info" label** on organic posts with "photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered," and "may apply penalties" if creators skip it ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/)). The Urdu narration is realistic-sounding synthetic audio, so **turn the label on for every Chai & Curiosity post.** ChatGPT images also carry hidden AI metadata, so Meta may add the label automatically anyway. Meta's documented reach penalties are tied to fact-check ratings and unoriginality, not to the AI label itself ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/)). Meta's originality rule dates from March 2026. It defines original content as "filmed or produced directly by" the Page owner, and says unoriginal Reels "will likely be deprioritized." Unoriginal means re-uploads, stitched clips, narration over existing footage without added value, and "low-value changes" like borders or captions. Repeat offenders can be made "non-recommendable" ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/)). Chai & Curiosity's own scripted, painted and animated Reels qualify as original. The risks are leaning too heavily on archive photos, posting the same render twice, and visible watermarks from other tools ([Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content)). The owner should check **Professional Dashboard** for any recommendation or originality notice now, because a hidden "non-recommendable" flag would also explain 3–5 impressions.

## What the winners in each niche actually publish

The clearest model for Chai & Curiosity is **Zem TV**, a Pakistan-based explainer channel with **6.14M YouTube subscribers and about 15 uploads every 28 days**. Its top videos all follow one pattern: a famous, concrete subject plus an unanswered "why/how". Examples are "How Einstein's Brain was Different?" (15.2M), "Deepest Hole in Earth" (14.7M), "Hidden Secrets of Taj Mahal" (14.6M) and "Why Dubai's Man Made Islands are Still Empty" (14.5M). Its latest, "Why Has Zamzam Well Never Dried Up?", passed **1.1M views in 34 hours** ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@zemtv.official/)). Its titles are in English even though it narrates in Urdu. Dedicated Urdu *science* channels are strikingly small: the legacy Science Digest Pakistan has about 3,100 YouTube subscribers, and Pakistan's science-and-tech rankings contain no Urdu storytelling science channel ([HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/top-youtube-science-technology-pakistan/)). Proven demand next to an empty niche is the opening. No Facebook Page follower counts for these channels could be verified, so the Facebook-specific comparison rests on their YouTube numbers. The main competition is Hindi "facts" channels in the same spoken language. Differentiate with a Nastaliq headline band, Pakistani examples, and Islamic-heritage science, which Hindi channels rarely cover.

For Margin of Safety the niche is mature. Real Engineering (5.1M) and Plainly Difficult (1.1M) gained **zero net subscribers in the last 28 days**, while Mentour Pilot added 30,000 with 10 uploads and Fascinating Horror added 10,000 with 5 ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@mentourpilot/), [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@plainlydifficult/)). The best model for a small, high-craft channel is **Brick Immortar**: **403K subscribers from only 55 videos**, and maritime investigations like SS El Faro (4.93M) built from Coast Guard and NTSB reports ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@brickimmortar/)). The cautionary tale is **Disaster Strucks**, a 2025 Shorts-only disaster-clip channel. It hit 1.25 billion views but turned only about 0.025% of them into subscribers, and it stalled from mid-2026 ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@disasterstrucks/)). Practical Engineering gets **0% of its views from Shorts** and is still one of the biggest channels in the niche ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/practicalengineeringchannel)). The leaders are faceless, single-narrator and investigation-led, which matches Margin of Safety's tone. The difference is that they use human voices, and no successful fully AI-voiced engineering channel was found.

## Length, timing and cadence for these two audiences

On Facebook the **43-second average watch on a 4-minute Reel** says viewers leave in the first minute. Facebook now accepts Reels of any length ([Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/making-it-easier-to-create-videos-on-facebook)), but third-party guidance puts the completion peak at 15–30 seconds ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/facebook-reels-time-limit-strategy/)). Completion is one of Meta's named ranking predictions. Making **45–90 seconds the default** turns a 43-second watch into a near-complete view. Longer vertical pieces should be kept for topics that have already proven themselves, with a new hook every 20–30 seconds. Pakistan has **70.6M Facebook users, 75% male, with 25–34 the largest group (40.8%)** ([NapoleonCat](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/facebook-users-in-pakistan/)). Facebook's reach there grew 15.5% in a year while YouTube's dipped ([DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-pakistan)), which supports a Facebook-first plan for Urdu. No Pakistan-specific best-time study exists. The best proxy is the **national internet peak of 7–11 pm**, when networks slow from congestion ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/11/04/heres-why-internet-slows-down-between-7pm-and-11pm/amp/)). Global Reels data also names 9 pm as the best hour ([DashSocial](https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-facebook)). Post at **about 9 pm PKT**, after Isha, which also catches Gulf diaspora evenings. Once the Page has followers, switch to the times Page Insights shows they are online. During Ramadan, move to after Taraweeh.

On YouTube the evidence on the Shorts mix conflicts, and the plan should say so. An 18,000-channel study found that **science and tech channels do best long-only or with Shorts at 25–40% of uploads**. Long-form suffered above about 55% Shorts. The same study found that a Short's conversion into long-form viewers rose from **0.04% to 0.5%** when the Related Video link was set and the Short ended with a spoken pointer ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)). That data comes mostly from established channels, though. The new-channel data says Shorts are how a zero-subscriber channel gets tested at all. The sensible compromise for the **first 90 days** is Shorts as discovery, each one a self-contained 30–50 second "one-fact near-miss" that points to a documentary. Count success in long-form views and subscribers, not Shorts views, and cut the Shorts share if long-form stops growing. Long documentaries in the niche run about 18 minutes at the top ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/practicalengineeringchannel)). **10–20 minutes** is enough for sourced depth without the 30–60 minute runtimes that need an established audience. The notes contain no posting-time data for an English engineering audience. Search-driven views care less about timing, so pick one fixed slot and adjust from the Audience tab. A slot around 10 pm–midnight PKT, which is UK early evening and US midday, is a reasonable untested starting point.

## Topics with real demand right now

For Chai & Curiosity, demand evidence comes from three places. The first is Zem TV's view counts. The second is Google's 2025 Pakistan Year in Search, which shows AI tools (Gemini, DeepSeek, Claude), floods and health questions like diabetes and cholesterol rising sharply ([Dawn Images](https://images.dawn.com/news/1194542), [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2581212/cricket-dramastop-google-searches-in-pakistan)). The third is the seasonal calendar: Lahore smog now, the Ramadan moon question in early 2027, and floods in July–September. Google's list ranks sudden rises, not total volume. Check each topic in Google Trends (Pakistan) and YouTube autocomplete before producing it.

For Margin of Safety, the strongest evidence is what already wins in the niche: Concorde (16.2M on Mentour Pilot), Taum Sauk dam (11M), Sunshine Skyway (4.71M) and El Faro (4.93M). The calendar adds two rare hooks: the **60th anniversary of the Apollo 1 fire on 27 Jan 2027**, which falls in NASA's annual Day of Remembrance week ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/feature/55-years-ago-the-apollo-1-fire-and-its-aftermath)), and the **40th anniversary of the Herald of Free Enterprise capsizing at Zeebrugge on 6 Mar 2027** ([Wikipedia](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeebrugge_Disaster)). Most other anniversary dates below come from general knowledge and must be confirmed before scheduling. Tenerife's 50th on 27 Mar 2027 would be the biggest, but aviation channels already cover it heavily.

## Money comes later, and the YouTube bar is about to double

**YouTube's Partner Program is available in Pakistan.** Until 31 Jan 2027, a channel needs **1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)). New applicants from **1 Feb 2027 need 8,000 hours or 20M Shorts views** ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)). Margin of Safety will realistically face the higher bar. The lower 500-subscriber "expanded" tier for fan-funding features **excludes Pakistan** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240)). Long documentaries are the only realistic route, because 8,000 hours is far closer than 20M Shorts views for a niche English channel. That is another reason to judge Shorts by how many viewers they send to the documentaries.

**Facebook Content Monetization is invite-only** and lists no thresholds or countries ([Meta for Creators](https://creators.facebook.com/tools/facebook-content-monetization)). Pakistani press reports a "300K views in 28 days" bar ([ARY](https://arynews.tv/facebook-lowers-monetization-threshold-for-new-creators)), but Meta does not publish one. The bigger obstacle is that since March 2025, **Pakistani bank accounts and tax details have not been accepted for payouts**, and many Pakistani Pages were demonetized ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/03/17/facebook-quietly-tightens-monetization-policy-in-pakistan/)). Using another country's bank or tax details that the owner does not legitimately hold would break Meta's rules and risk a permanent ban. **Facebook Stars**, in which viewers buy and send small gifts, has been available in Pakistan since 2022 ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2390281/meta-launches-facebook-stars-in-pakistan)). It is the only realistic Facebook income for now.

## Conclusion

Both accounts look like content problems but are really sample-size problems. With 1 follower and 0 subscribers, neither has shown the feeds enough fresh, original attempts for a breakout to be statistically likely. That changes what to measure for the next 90 days. Skip views per video. Track the Facebook retention curve and shares, and YouTube's "viewed vs swiped away" and long-form views per upload, against the account's own earlier videos. The biggest structural advantage is on Facebook: proven Urdu demand for "famous thing + why" stories, almost no dedicated Urdu science storytellers, and a Facebook audience in Pakistan that is still growing. On YouTube the advantage is narrower and time-limited: two round anniversaries in the next 100 days and an under-used "near-miss and the fix" angle in a crowded niche.

The main unknowns are worth testing directly. Nobody has measured how much a synthetic voice costs either channel against a human one. If retention stalls after 20–30 uploads despite good hooks, record the owner's own voice on one or two videos and compare. That is the cheapest experiment with the largest possible payoff. Monetization timelines also mean that speed matters less than consistency here. Neither account will plausibly reach payout thresholds before the YouTube bar doubles on 1 Feb 2027. A cadence the owner can sustain for six months beats a sprint that burns out in six weeks.
