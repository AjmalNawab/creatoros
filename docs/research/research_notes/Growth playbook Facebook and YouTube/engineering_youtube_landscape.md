# English engineering-disaster / engineering-story YouTube landscape (research notes, 2026-11-24)

Scope: which channels in the niche grow, their long-video and Shorts patterns, faceless/AI viability after the 2025-2026 policy changes, topic demand and anniversaries Dec 2026 to Mar 2027, and Shorts strategy for "Margin of Safety" (0 subs, 3 videos, Shorts ~71% stayed-to-watch).

Data caveat: all subscriber/view numbers are third-party tracker snapshots (vidIQ, Social Blade, CreatorDB etc.). vidIQ snapshots fetched 24 Nov 2026 are the most current and consistent; older snapshots are labelled with their date. Revenue figures are vidIQ models, not reported income.

## 1. Which channels are successful, and how fast did they (and newer ones) grow?

### Takeaway
The niche is mature: the big explainers (Real Engineering ~5.1M, Practical Engineering ~4.8M, The B1M ~4.0M, Mentour Pilot ~2.55M) and the disaster narrators (Fascinating Horror ~1.47M, Plainly Difficult ~1.1M) are large but several are now flat month to month. Growth is still happening where channels go deep and polished (Brick Immortar: ~403K subscribers from only 55 videos) or publish often in a sub-niche with a strong identity (Mentour Pilot +30K/28 days, Fascinating Horror +10K/28 days).

### Inferences
- Steady growth in 2026 comes from (a) high cadence plus a narrow, recognisable sub-niche (Mentour Pilot, 10 uploads/28 days) or (b) very high per-video production value at low cadence (Brick Immortar, ~1.1M views per video on average across 55 videos). The mid-cadence generalist narrators (Plainly Difficult, Real Engineering) are currently flat on net subscribers, which suggests the broad "disaster explainer" space is saturated at the top. A new channel needs a distinct angle; "near-miss and the fix", sourced from investigation reports, is less crowded than pure tragedy narration.
- Brick Immortar is the best comparable for a newer, low-volume, high-craft channel: it went from about 160K to about 400K between late 2022 and late 2026 with a handful of videos per year.
- Disaster Strucks shows that a Shorts-only disaster-clip channel can hit huge views quickly (1.25B in under 2 years), but it converted only ~0.025% of views into subscribers (307K / 1.25B) and then collapsed in 2026. Whether that was a policy action or the creator stopping is unknown (see section 3).

## 2. Long-video lengths, cadence, title/thumbnail formulas, narration, Shorts use

### Takeaway
Long videos in the niche run roughly 15–20+ minutes for explainers (Practical Engineering averages ~18 minutes) and longer for deep-dive disaster documentaries. Cadence ranges from weekly (Fascinating Horror, Plainly Difficult) to about 2–3 a month (Practical/Real Engineering) to a few a year (Brick Immortar). The biggest names barely use Shorts (Practical Engineering: 0% of views from Shorts). Titles name the event plus a hook ("The Wild Story of…", "The Crash that KILLED…", "… A Short Documentary").

### Cited Findings
- Practical Engineering: recent uploads average about 18:06; ~0.6 uploads/week; 100% of views from long-form, 0% from Shorts. Top videos are "The Bizarre Paths of Groundwater Around Structures" (14.0M), "How Do Sinkholes Form?" (13.0M), "The Hidden Engineering of Landfills" (11.0M), "Why Concrete Needs Reinforcement" (11.0M) and "The Wild Story of the Taum Sauk Dam Failure" (11.0M) — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/practicalengineeringchannel).
- Fascinating Horror: weekly uploads titled "… | A Short Documentary", about historical accidents and disasters; spin-off channel "Fascinating Failures" covers non-fatal mistakes — [TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/WebVideo/FascinatingHorror); [archive listing](https://archive.ventilaar.net/channel/UCFXad0mx4WxY1fXdbvtg0CQ). About 5 uploads in 28 days — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCFXad0mx4WxY1fXdbvtg0CQ).
- Plainly Difficult: 4 uploads in 28 days; title pattern "Crash at Over 110 MPH | The Santiago de Compostela Derailment" (stakes hook | event name) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@plainlydifficult/).
- Mentour Pilot: about 10 uploads in 28 days; titles use emotive caps ("The Crash that KILLED Concorde", "Pilots Make HUGE Mistake on Takeoff!") — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@mentourpilot/).
- Brick Immortar: 55 videos in ~7 years, ~1 upload/month recently; caps-hook titles ("DEADLY INDIFFERENCE: The Tragic Sinking of Miss Majestic"); the channel's disclaimer mentions the DoD, Coast Guard and NTSB, which signals investigation-report sourcing — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@brickimmortar/); [SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/brickimmortar).
- Mustard: little-known aviation/engineering topics, polished animated presentation, sponsored by Nebula (6 videos) — [SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/mustardchannel); [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/aviation-history-youtube-channels-best-examples-by-format/).
- Packaging advice (Paddy Galloway, via summaries; check against originals): YouTube is a click-and-watch platform, so titles and thumbnails are about half of success; titles of ~50 characters or fewer; at most three focal points in a thumbnail; open a curiosity gap; draft the title and thumbnail before production and test several concepts — [Colin and Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway); [Transcript Desk](https://thetranscriptdesk.substack.com/p/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025-edited); [opentools summary](https://opentools.ai/youtube-summary/the-secret-strategist-behind-the-biggest-youtubers).

### Inferences
- Title formula that works across the niche: **[stakes or curiosity hook] + [named event or object]**, e.g. "The 1 Typo That Lost a Mars Lander | Mars Polar Lander". Event names carry search demand, and the hook carries the click from Browse.
- Thumbnails (inferred from the top-video titles; not visually verified this session): one dominant object (bridge, aircraft, ship) at the moment of failure, plus 2–4 words or none.
- Narration in the leaders is calm, single-narrator and investigation-led (Plainly Difficult, Fascinating Horror and Brick Immortar are all faceless voice-over channels). That matches Margin of Safety's tone. The difference is that those channels use human voices.
- For a new channel, 10–20 minutes is a reasonable long-form target: long enough for sourced depth, and shorter than the 30–60+ minute Brick Immortar format, which needs established trust.

## 3. Is faceless/AI-narrated content succeeding post-2025? Policy impact

### Takeaway
Faceless voice-over documentaries clearly still succeed (Fascinating Horror, Plainly Difficult and Brick Immortar are all faceless). The risk is not being faceless; it is being "mass-produced, repetitive or template-based", which YouTube made explicitly non-monetizable in July 2025 and clarified further in July 2026. No verified example of a successful fully AI-voiced engineering channel was found.

### Cited Findings
- 15 July 2025: YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content", clarifying that it covers mass-produced and repetitive content. YouTube described this as a clarification of an existing rule — [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules).
- July 2026: YouTube further clarified the policy into three non-monetizable categories: generic, repetitive or template-based content; off-putting or distressing content; and AI personas used to discuss revenue — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-cracks-down-on-unsatisfying-or-off-putting-videos-with-demonetization-3387786/).
- Sources disagree on scope. Some say the policy is not a crackdown on faceless or commentary channels as such; others claim that a visible human host is a ranking signal for authenticity. YouTube says AI-assisted content is not penalised for carrying an AI label, and it gave no count of affected channels — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-cant-tell-a-directed-ai-film-from-a-bot-farm).
- Marketers report concern among creators about the crackdown — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-has-creators-concerned-marketers-cheering).
- Vendor blogs (low reliability) say few faceless documentary channels succeed, and that documentary formats earn high watch time and an RPM of around $12.6 — [Autonolab](https://blog.autonolab.com/niches/2025-11-29-faceless-youtube-documentary/); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-growth-strategy/).
- Possible casualty, not confirmed: Disaster Strucks (disaster-clip Shorts, created Jan 2025) stopped gaining subscribers after June 2026 and has not uploaded in 28 days — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@disasterstrucks/).

### Inferences
- Margin of Safety's protective factors under the policy: original research from cited investigation reports, a unique script per video, custom animated diagrams, and a consistent editorial voice. Its risks: a TTS voice (`en-GB-RyanNeural`), templated scene structure across videos, and reused visual templates. Mitigations: show sources on screen, vary structure, add an on-camera or recorded human voice element if feasible, and disclose AI use where YouTube requires it.
- The "off-putting or distressing" category matters for disaster content. Near-miss and fix framing (calm, no gore, no victim exploitation) is safer than tragedy-forward framing.
- The Disaster Strucks timing (stall from mid-2026) coincides with the July 2026 clarification. That is suggestive only.

### Gaps
- No named, verified AI-voiced engineering or disaster channel with growth data was found, and no named channel confirmed as demonetized under the policy.
- Whether TTS narration is itself treated as a negative signal is unconfirmed.

## 5. Recommended Shorts length and frequency, and Shorts-to-long-form conversion

### Takeaway
Evidence says Shorts reach new channels faster but convert poorly unless deliberately bridged. In science and tech, long-only channels match or beat mixed channels, so Shorts should support long-form rather than replace it: roughly 25–40% of uploads at most (about 1–3 Shorts per long video), self-contained, with Related Video links set. Optimal length data is weak; most Shorts run 20–40 seconds, and the maximum is now 3 minutes.

### Cited Findings
- AIR Media-Tech, 18,000 English channels, Mar 2025 – Mar 2026, correlational: the best mix is 25–40% Shorts; long-form suffers above ~55% Shorts. **Science & tech: Long-Only leads** (at 1M–10M, Long-Only medians are 16% higher; at 100K–1M roughly equal), so default to long-form and make any Shorts self-contained. **Education: mixed channels have 37% more subscribers at 100K–1M**, with a median Shorts share of ~38%. Shorts-to-long conversion reportedly rises from **0.04% to 0.5%** when the Related Video field is set and a verbal CTA is used. 1,247 channels that shifted sharply toward Shorts did worse — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels); [AIR 2026](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/should-you-chase-shorts-views-or-double-down-on-long-form-for-channel-growth).
- vidIQ first-10-videos study (448K channels created Jan–Jun 2025): median 30-day views were Shorts ~1,275–1,362 per upload versus long-form 180–272; long-form views grew ~51% from the 1st to the 10th upload while Shorts did not grow. Best of the first ten reached 10K views for 48% of Shorts channels versus 30.8% of long-form channels. Subscribers were not measured — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/).
- Overseer (27,581 videos): viral Shorts do not automatically lead to long-form success, though strong performance in both often co-occurs on the same channels — [Overseer](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/your-shorts-are-viral-will-long-videos-follow).
- Little Dot (800+ channels): average views per video converged across short, long and ultra-long formats by 2025 — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/02/12/little-dot-youtube-video-length-whitepaper-viewer-habits-2026).
- The Shorts maximum was extended from 60 seconds to 3 minutes in October 2024; most Shorts run 20–40 seconds — [Piktochart, via search snippet](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/) (page fetch blocked).
- Example of poor conversion: Disaster Strucks, ~307K subs from 1.25B views (≈0.025% subs per view), then stalled — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@disasterstrucks/).
- Practical Engineering gets 0% of views from Shorts and is still one of the largest channels in the niche — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/practicalengineeringchannel).

### Inferences
- For Margin of Safety: 2–3 original vertical Shorts per long video, about 30–50 seconds (the 71% stayed-to-watch rate is a good signal at the current length; don't stretch to 3 minutes). Each Short should be a self-contained "one-fact near-miss" (the single error, the moment it was caught or missed), with the Related Video set to the full documentary and a one-line verbal pointer at the end.
- Use Shorts mainly as discovery while long-form views are tiny (a long-form median of ~200 views/upload for new channels is normal), and judge success by long-form views and subscribers, not Shorts views.
- Keep Shorts at or below ~40% of uploads to avoid the "shift to Shorts" penalty pattern.
- Expect Shorts subscriber conversion around 0.03–0.5% of views (estimate based on AIR's range and the Disaster Strucks example).

### Gaps
- No niche-specific (engineering/disaster) data on optimal Shorts length or Shorts-viewer to long-viewer conversion.
- No first-party data from successful niche channels on whether their Shorts are clips or originals.
- The Metricool 2026 Shorts/long-form study exists ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/03/youtube-shorts-long-form-study-metricool/)) but was not read.
